# AGENTS

- The protocol is `protocol/`: one short document per part. A change to a format touches four things together, or it isn't done: its document there, its schema, its vectors, and `@nswp/core`. `packages/core/test/docs.test.ts` checks the documents against the library.
- `spec.md` is the full design and the reasoning. Its section numbers (§) are cited from code in three repos, so never renumber it. What changed goes in `CHANGELOG.md`, newest first, not in the spec.
- Anything two implementations must agree on to work together belongs in `protocol/`, not only in code. If you add a message, a tag or a field, write it down there in the same change.
- Before finishing a change: `npm test`, `npm run vectors:check`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`.
- Vectors are generated: edit `scripts/generate-vectors.ts` (hand-written expectations), then `npm run vectors`. The generator refuses to write if core disagrees.
- Schemas must never reject input that `@nswp/core` accepts (warnings included); `packages/core/test/schemas.test.ts` enforces it.
- Issue codes are lowercase snake_case and stable (BitBlocks convention D4). Unknown values are warnings, not errors.
- Never commit keys. The reference publisher key is at `~/.config/nswp/reference-publisher.key`. Test vector keys are deliberately public and must never appear in a real manifest.
- Publishing events to public relays and deploying the sites are outward-facing: the repo owner approves each.
- Part I of the spec must not depend on BitBlocks (spec §1.3).
- **The Bridge (`browser/bridge.md`, `packages/bridge`, `packages/core/src/bridge.ts`, `nswp-sdk/terminal`) runs commands on a person's computer.** Its document, its schema, its vectors and the code change together. Never without the owner's decision: make it answer more than one key, skip or pre-answer the question at the computer, lengthen a grant, let a grant and a pass share a kind or be accepted for each other, accept a grant twice, reply to strangers more often, or let a world reach any of it. One exception stands, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-18: a world they are building may be handed a world grant (kind 27303) when it is on the list in their home, their Bridge was started naming it, and the computer is asked each time. Never widen it: no other way onto either list, no remembering a world between runs of the Bridge, no longer life, no signer or owner's key in a world, and no world grant made or accepted for a world the owner did not name. Test it with throwaway keys and a relay on this machine only (`packages/bridge/test/relay.ts`); never run it against a real identity or a public relay from automation, and never give a test a config folder that isn't a temporary one (`--dir`).
- **Speech (`packages/sdk/src/speech.ts`, `packages/bridge/src/ears.ts`):** what a visitor says is turned into text on their own computer, or by their own browser after they have been told where the sound may go. Never send sound to a service of ours or anyone's, never keep it past the moment it is used, never skip the telling, and never let spoken words press Enter or any other key: they are typed as one line and the visitor decides.
- **Staying joined (NSWP-02, NSWP-03, spec §9.4):** `joined=1` and `mic=1` on a link are a claim anyone can write, and `joined` and `mic` in the arrival context are the visitor's own client's word. Never let a world act on the link, its own address or anything but the arrival context; never join a private room from either; never let a microphone travel without joined; and never make a client pass `mic` on without the visitor's standing say-so in that client (off until given). A visitor who arrives joined is told at once, with leaving one press away. Change the flags and their documents, the arrival schema, its vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **Music shared with people nearby (`RoomSession.setMusic`/`listen`, `musicToHear`):** both halves are off until the visitor turns them on. Never without the owner's decision: turn hearing on for anyone, send music to a member who hasn't asked for it or is out of range, raise the range or the number heard at once, let a world turn sharing on for its visitors or play one visitor's music to another by any other road, or send it through a server. A world may switch it off, never on.
- **Sound (`packages/sdk/src/sound.ts`, `sound-store.ts`):** the cues are a closed list; a world replaces or silences them and can't add to it. Never without the owner's decision: let a world set a visitor's volumes or play above the limiter, fetch a sound from any site but the world's own, play anything before the visitor's first touch, send one visitor's sound triggers to another, or upload, publish or share a visitor's own songs. Their songs stay in their browser and only they hear them.
- **The kit's own sound files (`packages/sdk/sounds/`):** the cues, the hum and the music every world starts with are files the owner chose by ear (2026-11-18), with the ones made in the browser kept as what plays when a file can't be had. Never add, replace or remove one without the owner having heard it. Every file is listed in that folder's README with what made it and under what terms; nothing goes in whose terms for redistribution are unclear unless the owner has settled them (as they did for YuE2, on 2026-11-18, recorded in that README), and nothing taken or imitated from a recording, a library, a show or an artist's work. Keep each form small: these ship inside every world.
- **A shared screen in a room (`RoomSession.setScreen`/`watch`, `addRoomControls`):** offering one sends nothing; it goes only to a member who has asked to watch, and stops going when they stop asking. Never without the owner's decision: open someone's screen in front of a visitor who didn't choose it, send it to everyone, keep sending after the sharer stops or leaves, record it, or share sound with it. The sharer is always told how many are watching.
- **Blocks that travel (NSWP-02, NSWP-03):** `blocked` on a link is a world's report and a claim anyone can write; `blocked` in the arrival context is the visitor's own client's list. Never let a world act on the link, add to a visitor's list without their seeing who and saying yes, take anyone off a list because a link said so, keep an arrival's list in a world, or carry a guest's key. A world applies the arrival's list from the first moment of a visit. Change the field and its documents, the arrival schema, its vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **Blocking in rooms (`packages/sdk/src/blocks.ts`, `RoomSession.block`):** a block is carried out on the blocker's own device and nobody is told. Never publish a block, send a block list to a relay, a world's server or another member, or tell the blocked person. Only people who came by name are kept between visits (their identity key, on that site); never keep guests' session keys as if blocking them could last. A blocked key is not answered, whatever it sends.
- **Casual and intense (`packages/sdk/src/fps.ts`, `input.ts`, NSWP-02 "How a visitor moves"):** two ways of moving, with the same two names at a desk and in a headset. Intense at a desk: the mouse looks, keys walk, Shift runs, F uses, Escape is the menu. Intense in a headset: walking and turning smoothly, the view not narrowed. Casual is what a visitor gets until they choose, or their own client says they had chosen. Never without the owner's decision: make intense the default anywhere, let a choice made at a desk change how a headset moves (smooth movement makes some people ill), take the pointer before the visitor has pressed anything (the mouse looks at once on arriving, by its own movement over the view, and the pointer is taken at their first key or click: the owner's decision of 2026-11-18), keep it while the menu is open, swallow Escape so that a visitor can't get their pointer back, or offer the desk's intense way where there is no mouse. `moving` on a link and in the arrival is a preference only: never hang anything else on it. Change the flag and its documents, the arrival schema, its vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **Other engines (`packages/sdk/src/engine.ts`, `engines/`, `docs/other-engines.md`):** `engines/nswp-engine.js` is built from the source (`npm run build:engine`) and committed, with a copy inside the Godot addon; `npm run engine:check` fails when they are stale. It stays free of rendering and of anything evaluated from text, and every function takes and returns text. An engine's side never reads the page's address itself: only what `NSWP.arrival()` gives. Say plainly in the addon's README what has been run in the engine and what hasn't. The Godot example (`engines/godot/example/`) is exported with `npm run build:godot`, which needs Godot 4 and its web templates; its export (`engines/godot/dist/`) is never committed. Keep the `.gd.uid` files Godot writes beside each script. The script also carries the kit's own menu and sounds (`engine-menu.ts`): what a visitor already knows is shared, never redrawn, so add to the menu in `ui/` or `sound.ts` and let every engine have it. The Godot doorway's frame (`gate/frame.obj`) and the addon's copy of the sounds are made by `npm run build:engine`: never edit them in place. Its three shaders (`gate/*.gdshader`) are the kit's (`alloy.ts`, and the veil and glow in `home.ts`) written for Godot: change one, change the other. The numbers in `nswp.gd`'s `kit` must match `NSWP.kit()`; a test checks.
- **The visitor's console and the summons (NSWP-11, `ui/summon.ts`, `ui/hud.ts`):** the summons is the visitor's and nobody else's. Never bind the key left of 1, a two-finger hold or a held B/Y to anything else in the kit or in a reference world; never let a presence level, `claimInput`, a key setting or a world's option switch it off; never leave anything on screen after a hidden console is put away; and always give the keys back to what had them. The console is drawn above everything a page can draw. If a new kind of device comes along, it gets a summons before it gets anything else. Don't describe any of this as a layer above a world: the world's page draws it, and the docs say so.
- **The integration guide (`docs/integrating.md`):** the one page that says what is required of a world and what is a default it may replace. When something is added to the kit, it goes in one list or the other there, with the one thing that must stay true if it is replaced. Signing in is never on either list for a world: it is the visitor's client's, and a world never shows one.
- **The probe (`scripts/probe-world.mjs`):** it tries a world as a visitor would and says "works", "fails", or nothing. "fails" is only for a page that didn't load or threw before drawing anything: never for something a software renderer or a made-up headset might get wrong. It visits and nothing more: it never signs in, never presses more than a tap and a way into VR, and sends nothing anywhere.
- **Signing in from inside a world (NSWP-10; `who=1`, `portal.signIn()`, `ui/identity.ts`, `NSWP.signIn`, `Nswp.sign_in`):** a world's control only leaves for the visitor's own client and comes back. Never make the kit, the engine script or a reference world draw a sign-in, take a credential, open the client's page in a frame or a panel, or show a name that didn't come from a checked pass (`arrival.identity()`). `who=1` is a request anyone can write: never let it, or anything else on a link, sign someone in, choose "by name" for them, or skip the check of the door. The control says which host it leads to, and is absent when there is no Airlock to go back to. A world's own door is what its manifest lists (`doorFor`), never a guess.
- **One order for every menu and taskbar (`STANDARD_ORDER` and `STANDARD` in `ui/model.ts`, NSWP-11 "What is in it"):** the things every place has are ordered by the kit, not by where a world's code added them, so the first key is the way home in every world. Never put a world's own control ahead of them, give a reference world, the template and the engine script different sets of the standard things, or add a standard thing to one and not the others. A new standard control goes in `STANDARD_ORDER`, in NSWP-11, and in all three.
- **A look is changed in the visitor's own client and nowhere else (NSWP-05 "Changing a look"; `who=look`, `portal.changeLook()`, `figureAction`):** never give a world, the kit or the engine script a way to edit a look or to send one out on a link, and never make a look a travelling setting: a look a world could write is a mark it could follow a visitor by.
- **Signed in everywhere (NSWP-10):** a client may send a signed-in visitor by name to every world that has proved who it is. Never to one that hasn't, never on a link's word, and never without the way out beside the sign-in.
- **Drawing the visitor's things yourself (NSWP-11 "Drawing it yourself"; `controls.view/run`, `NSWP.view/run/open`):** a world may draw any of them anywhere, in any style. They are pressed by id and are the console's own: never give a world a second "home" that isn't `portal.home()`, a volume that isn't the visitor's, or a way to press one that skips what the console's does. When a control's id changes, change the table in NSWP-11: worlds press them by name.
- **Fullscreen on arriving (`ui/fullscreen.ts` `resumeFullscreen`):** it is looked for in `arrival.read()`, before anything is built, and taken at the first press of any kind. Don't move it later, and don't add a "press to begin" to get the press: a browser needs one and the protocol asks for none.
- **Settings that travel (NSWP-11, core `settings.ts`, sdk `settings.ts`):** a closed list of preferences that go with a visitor through a door and home: volumes, text size, motion, a headset's hands, the screen filled. Never add to it anything that would need asking about, or that gives a world something: a microphone, joined, blocks, hearing shared music, a pass, a look, an identity. Never carry a world's colours. `cleanSettings` is the only way in and out, and it drops what it doesn't know. A new setting gets a line in NSWP-11, the schema and a vector. Anything in the kit that keeps a visitor's setting begins from `arrivedSettings()` and reports with `reportSettings`.
- **The form a visitor is in (NSWP-11, `form.ts`):** `form` on a link and in an arrival is a preference, followed only where the device can do it. Never use it to decide anything but layout, never send it on the way home, and never let it start a headset session or stand in for `immersive`.
- **Marks on the floor, and the reach of a door (`floor-mark.ts`, `DOOR_REACH`, `TAP_REACH`):** the ring, the dot and the dotted line are for a flat screen moving the casual way, and are never sent to anyone else or drawn in a headset or the intense way. A door answers to being pointed at or pressed only within `DOOR_REACH`; never make a door go through from further off, and never let F or a click go through one moving the intense way. `TAP_REACH` was raised from three metres to six on 2026-11-18 because the floor nearer than about 2.8 metres is below the bottom of the screen, so the marks couldn't be seen. The owner confirmed six metres the same day: don't change it without their decision.
- **Through a door (NSWP-02 "The chamber"):** a door chosen from where the visitor stands brings the crossing's room up; a doorway walked into does not, and they go at once. Don't bring anything up round someone who is already walking through. The room at the other end is the same either way.
- **The place's music has a name (`pieceName`, `sounds.piece`):** the Sound section says which piece is playing, by its file's name, and goes on to the next when pressed. Name music files so they can be told apart, and never rename one without saying so: the owner points pieces out by these names.
- **Running and jumping (`stroll.ts` `RUN`, `jump.ts`, `actions.ts`):** every way of moving has both, and they are one run and one jump: `RUN` is the only number for how much faster a run is, and `addJump` the only jump. A new way of moving gets both or says in the docs why it can't (a headset's casual steps have no run). A jump never changes where a visitor may stand. Keep the table in `docs/controls-kit.md` ("Running and jumping") true.
- **Footsteps (`stepShape` in `sound-store.ts`):** a step is quieter than any other cue and never the same twice. That is done as each is played, not with more files: no sound file is added, swapped or removed without the owner having heard it.
- **Local copies (`packages/core/src/local.ts`, `scripts/serve-local.mjs`, `docs/local-server.md`):** on a development server a page follows a map, real address to local copy, when it sends the browser on. Three things must stay true: only a page on a local address over plain http asks for a map; a map can only lead to a local address; and only the last step (the navigation) is changed. Never use the map for what is checked, shown as the destination, signed, or granted: an Airlock still verifies the real site, and a grant to a computer (BitPortal's "bring") is for the real address alone. An Airlock that sends a visitor to a copy says so. Anything that sends the browser to another site goes through `toLocal`.
- **Signs (`packages/sdk/src/sign.ts`, `controls.setTarget`):** a sign is a size in a visitor's view, not in the world. On a flat screen, say what is pointed at beside the controls (`setTarget(name, detail)`), not on a sign hung in front of it. In the world, scale a sign by distance every frame (`viewScale`). Don't add a sign of fixed size that a visitor can walk up to.
- **The crossing's room (`packages/sdk/src/chamber.ts`):** it is drawn last, on a cleared depth (`CHAMBER_ORDER`), so that nothing a world or another visitor has standing there shows inside it. Don't give anything else a draw order at or above it but the visitor's own things (`keep`), and don't draw other people while it stands.
- **Moving on a flat screen (`packages/sdk/src/stroll.ts`):** a tap or click is a walk at the keys' pace to a spot `TAP_REACH` metres off at most. Don't turn it back into a jump or lengthen it without the owner's decision. In a headset a step stays a jump: a slide there makes people ill.
- **Who a world is for (NSWP-01, NSWP-08, spec §15):** `audience` is `kids`, `general` or `adult`, and `audienceOf` reads a warning with no audience as adult. Never make that reading more trusting (a warned, unrated world taken as fine for everyone), never let `adult` be announced without `content-warning`, and never describe a rating as a safeguard on its own: it is the maker's word, and the safeguard is a host's list. Change the tag and its document, vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **A home someone starts in (NSWP-07, spec §14.1):** `home` in a manifest's capabilities, `doors=1` on an Airlock with no destination, and `home` in the arrival context belong together: change one and the other two, their documents, the arrival schema and its vectors change with it. A world that says it can be a home must keep the way to the visitor's doors wherever it offers the way home.
- `browser/spec.md` is a plan for a browser, not part of the protocol. A world must never need that browser: nothing in `protocol/` may depend on it, and no announcement field is added for it until one exists to read it.
