# AGENTS

- The protocol is `protocol/`: one short document per part. A change to a format touches four things together, or it isn't done: its document there, its schema, its vectors, and `@nswp/core`. `packages/core/test/docs.test.ts` checks the documents against the library.
- `spec.md` is the full design and the reasoning. Its section numbers (§) are cited from code in three repos, so never renumber it. What changed goes in `CHANGELOG.md`, newest first, not in the spec.
- Anything two implementations must agree on to work together belongs in `protocol/`, not only in code. If you add a message, a tag or a field, write it down there in the same change.
- Before finishing a change: `npm test`, `npm run vectors:check`, `npm run typecheck`, `npm run build`.
- Vectors are generated: edit `scripts/generate-vectors.ts` (hand-written expectations), then `npm run vectors`. The generator refuses to write if core disagrees.
- Schemas must never reject input that `@nswp/core` accepts (warnings included); `packages/core/test/schemas.test.ts` enforces it.
- Issue codes are lowercase snake_case and stable (BitBlocks convention D4). Unknown values are warnings, not errors.
- Never commit keys. The reference publisher key is at `~/.config/nswp/reference-publisher.key`. Test vector keys are deliberately public and must never appear in a real manifest.
- Publishing events to public relays and deploying the sites are outward-facing: the repo owner approves each.
- Part I of the spec must not depend on BitBlocks (spec §1.3).
- **The Bridge (`browser/bridge.md`, `packages/bridge`, `packages/core/src/bridge.ts`, `nswp-sdk/terminal`) runs commands on a person's computer.** Its document, its schema, its vectors and the code change together. Never without the owner's decision: make it answer more than one key, skip or pre-answer the question at the computer, lengthen a grant, let a grant and a pass share a kind or be accepted for each other, accept a grant twice, reply to strangers more often, or let a world reach any of it. One exception stands, by the owner's decision of 2026-11-18: a world they are building may be handed a world grant (kind 27303) when it is on the list in their home, their Bridge was started naming it, and the computer is asked each time. Never widen it: no other way onto either list, no remembering a world between runs of the Bridge, no longer life, no signer or owner's key in a world, and no world grant made or accepted for a world the owner did not name. Test it with throwaway keys and a relay on this machine only (`packages/bridge/test/relay.ts`); never run it against a real identity or a public relay from automation, and never give a test a config folder that isn't a temporary one (`--dir`).
- **Speech (`packages/sdk/src/speech.ts`, `packages/bridge/src/ears.ts`):** what a visitor says is turned into text on their own computer, or by their own browser after they have been told where the sound may go. Never send sound to a service of ours or anyone's, never keep it past the moment it is used, never skip the telling, and never let spoken words press Enter or any other key: they are typed as one line and the visitor decides.
- **Staying joined (NSWP-02, NSWP-03, spec §9.4):** `joined=1` and `mic=1` on a link are a claim anyone can write, and `joined` and `mic` in the arrival context are the visitor's own client's word. Never let a world act on the link, its own address or anything but the arrival context; never join a private room from either; never let a microphone travel without joined; and never make a client pass `mic` on without the visitor's standing say-so in that client (off until given). A visitor who arrives joined is told at once, with leaving one press away. Change the flags and their documents, the arrival schema, its vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **Music shared with people nearby (`RoomSession.setMusic`/`listen`, `musicToHear`):** both halves are off until the visitor turns them on. Never without the owner's decision: turn hearing on for anyone, send music to a member who hasn't asked for it or is out of range, raise the range or the number heard at once, let a world turn sharing on for its visitors or play one visitor's music to another by any other road, or send it through a server. A world may switch it off, never on.
- **Sound (`packages/sdk/src/sound.ts`, `sound-store.ts`):** the cues are a closed list; a world replaces or silences them and can't add to it. Never without the owner's decision: let a world set a visitor's volumes or play above the limiter, fetch a sound from any site but the world's own, play anything before the visitor's first touch, send one visitor's sound triggers to another, or upload, publish or share a visitor's own songs. Their songs stay in their browser and only they hear them.
- **The kit's own sound files (`packages/sdk/sounds/`):** the cues, the hum and the music every world starts with are files the owner chose by ear (2026-11-18), with the ones made in the browser kept as what plays when a file can't be had. Never add, replace or remove one without the owner having heard it. Every file is listed in that folder's README with what made it and under what terms; nothing goes in whose terms for redistribution are unclear unless the owner has settled them (as they did for YuE2, on 2026-11-18, recorded in that README), and nothing taken or imitated from a recording, a library, a show or an artist's work. Keep each form small: these ship inside every world.
- **A shared screen in a room (`RoomSession.setScreen`/`watch`, `addRoomControls`):** offering one sends nothing; it goes only to a member who has asked to watch, and stops going when they stop asking. Never without the owner's decision: open someone's screen in front of a visitor who didn't choose it, send it to everyone, keep sending after the sharer stops or leaves, record it, or share sound with it. The sharer is always told how many are watching.
- **Blocks that travel (NSWP-02, NSWP-03):** `blocked` on a link is a world's report and a claim anyone can write; `blocked` in the arrival context is the visitor's own client's list. Never let a world act on the link, add to a visitor's list without their seeing who and saying yes, take anyone off a list because a link said so, keep an arrival's list in a world, or carry a guest's key. A world applies the arrival's list from the first moment of a visit. Change the field and its documents, the arrival schema, its vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **Blocking in rooms (`packages/sdk/src/blocks.ts`, `RoomSession.block`):** a block is carried out on the blocker's own device and nobody is told. Never publish a block, send a block list to a relay, a world's server or another member, or tell the blocked person. Only people who came by name are kept between visits (their identity key, on that site); never keep guests' session keys as if blocking them could last. A blocked key is not answered, whatever it sends.
- **Casual and intense (`packages/sdk/src/fps.ts`, `input.ts`, NSWP-02 "How a visitor moves"):** two ways of moving, with the same two names at a desk and in a headset. Intense at a desk: the mouse looks, keys walk, Shift runs, F uses, Escape is the menu. Intense in a headset: walking and turning smoothly, the view not narrowed. Casual is what a visitor gets until they choose, or their own client says they had chosen. Never without the owner's decision: make intense the default anywhere, let a choice made at a desk change how a headset moves (smooth movement makes some people ill), take the pointer before the visitor has pressed anything (the mouse looks at once on arriving, by its own movement over the view, and the pointer is taken at their first key or click: the owner's decision of 2026-11-18), keep it while the menu is open, swallow Escape so that a visitor can't get their pointer back, or offer the desk's intense way where there is no mouse. `moving` on a link and in the arrival is a preference only: never hang anything else on it. Change the flag and its documents, the arrival schema, its vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **Other engines (`packages/sdk/src/engine.ts`, `engines/`, `docs/other-engines.md`):** `engines/nswp-engine.js` is built from the source (`npm run build:engine`) and committed, with a copy inside the Godot addon; `npm run engine:check` fails when they are stale. It stays free of rendering and of anything evaluated from text, and every function takes and returns text. An engine's side never reads the page's address itself: only what `NSWP.arrival()` gives. Say plainly in the addon's README what has been run in the engine and what hasn't. The Godot example (`engines/godot/example/`) is exported with `npm run build:godot`, which needs Godot 4 and its web templates; its export (`engines/godot/dist/`) is never committed. Keep the `.gd.uid` files Godot writes beside each script. The script also carries the kit's own menu and sounds (`engine-menu.ts`): what a visitor already knows is shared, never redrawn, so add to the menu in `ui/` or `sound.ts` and let every engine have it. The Godot doorway's frame (`gate/frame.obj`) and the addon's copy of the sounds are made by `npm run build:engine`: never edit them in place. Its three shaders (`gate/*.gdshader`) are the kit's (`alloy.ts`, and the veil and glow in `home.ts`) written for Godot: change one, change the other. The numbers in `nswp.gd`'s `kit` must match `NSWP.kit()`; a test checks.
- **The visitor's console and the summons (NSWP-11, `ui/summon.ts`, `ui/hud.ts`):** the summons is the visitor's and nobody else's. Never bind the key left of 1, a two-finger hold or a held B/Y to anything else in the kit or in a reference world; never let a presence level, `claimInput`, a key setting or a world's option switch it off; never leave anything on screen after a hidden console is put away; and always give the keys back to what had them. The console is drawn above everything a page can draw. If a new kind of device comes along, it gets a summons before it gets anything else. Don't describe any of this as a layer above a world: the world's page draws it, and the docs say so.
- **The integration guide (`docs/integrating.md`):** the one page that says what is required of a world and what is a default it may replace. When something is added to the kit, it goes in one list or the other there, with the one thing that must stay true if it is replaced. Signing in is never on either list for a world: it is the visitor's client's, and a world never shows one.
- **The probe (`scripts/probe-world.mjs`):** it tries a world as a visitor would and says "works", "fails", or nothing. "fails" is only for a page that didn't load or threw before drawing anything: never for something a software renderer or a made-up headset might get wrong. It visits and nothing more: it never signs in, never presses more than a tap and a way into VR, and sends nothing anywhere.
- **Signing in from inside a world (NSWP-10; `who=1`, `portal.signIn()`, `ui/identity.ts`, `NSWP.signIn`, `Nswp.sign_in`):** a world's control only leaves for the visitor's own client and comes back. Never make the kit, the engine script or a reference world draw a sign-in, take a credential, open the client's page in a frame or a panel, or show a name that didn't come from a checked pass (`arrival.identity()`). `who=1` is a request anyone can write: never let it, or anything else on a link, sign someone in, choose "by name" for them, or skip the check of the door. The control says which host it leads to, and is absent when there is no Airlock to go back to. A world's own door is what its manifest lists (`doorFor`), never a guess.
- **The kit's version (`KIT_VERSION` in core `constants.ts`, `kit` in a manifest, NSWP-01, `docs/keeping-up.md`):** it is the spec's version, and a test fails until it matches the newest heading in `CHANGELOG.md`: change both together. A world's `kit` is only ever something to tell a visitor or an owner. Never let it gate a door, a verification, a crossing or a listing, never make it a warning, and never turn it into a reason to load code from somewhere else: the kit is copied into each world and is never fetched as a world runs. Do not add a hosted "always latest" copy of the kit, a loader for one, or a recommendation to use one, without the owner's decision.
- **One order for every menu and taskbar (`STANDARD_ORDER` and `STANDARD` in `ui/model.ts`, NSWP-11 "What is in it"):** the things every place has are ordered by the kit, not by where a world's code added them, so the first key is the way home in every world. Never put a world's own control ahead of them, give a reference world, the template and the engine script different sets of the standard things, or add a standard thing to one and not the others. A new standard control goes in `STANDARD_ORDER`, in NSWP-11, and in all three.
- **A look is changed in the visitor's own client and nowhere else (NSWP-05 "Changing a look"; `who=look`, `portal.changeLook()`, `figureAction`):** never give a world, the kit or the engine script a way to edit a look or to send one out on a link, and never make a look a travelling setting: a look a world could write is a mark it could follow a visitor by.
- **Signed in everywhere (NSWP-10):** a client may send a signed-in visitor by name to every world that has proved who it is. Never to one that hasn't, never on a link's word, and never without the way out beside the sign-in.
- **Drawing the visitor's things yourself (NSWP-11 "Drawing it yourself"; `controls.view/run`, `NSWP.view/run/open`):** a world may draw any of them anywhere, in any style. They are pressed by id and are the console's own: never give a world a second "home" that isn't `portal.home()`, a volume that isn't the visitor's, or a way to press one that skips what the console's does. When a control's id changes, change the table in NSWP-11: worlds press them by name.
- **Fullscreen on arriving (`ui/fullscreen.ts` `resumeFullscreen`):** it is looked for in `arrival.read()`, before anything is built, and taken at the first press of any kind. Don't move it later, and don't add a "press to begin" to get the press: a browser needs one and the protocol asks for none.
- **A room's own note signer (`addRoomControls` `notes`, `RoomNotes`):** for a visitor's own client alone, in its own room, where the visitor is already signed in to it. Never pass one from a world or a reference world, and never give the kit a default that holds a key: a world's notes are signed by the visitor's hidden signer and nothing else. Whatever signs, `postNote` checks the signature, the kind and the words before anything is sent; keep that check.
- **Where a visitor's signer is (NSWP-03 `signer`, NSWP-10; core `signerAt`, sdk `arrival.signer()`, `worldSigner`):** the visitor's client names it in the arrival, or it is beside the client's Airlock. Never put a signer's address in the kit, a world, or the engine script: which signer a visitor uses is theirs and their client's, and a world must work with any. The control names the signer's real address before it is pressed (`signer.host`), never a development server's copy: keep that, because an arrival is something anyone can write. The reference client names BitLogin's own site (BitPortal `INSTANCE.signer`), decided by the owner on 2026-11-19.
- **One sign-in in a world (NSWP-10 "One sign-in"; sdk `hereAs`, `byName`, `signerNamed`, `youCard`; owner's instruction of 2026-11-19: "It should not tell me that im here as [my nostr username] if its not actually signed in"):** where the visitor's client named a signer, a pass counts only while that signer is unlocked for the world and holds the pass's key. Never show "here as" a name, and never join a room by name, for someone signed out there. Never show a second sign-in row beside it.
- **Always the latest (NSWP-09 "They always get the latest"; core `fresh.ts`, sdk `fresh.ts`, `ui/fresh.ts`; asked for by the owner on 2026-11-20: "have the browser know if the site has been updated and always ... load the latest", "a documented requirement"):** every site built here sends `Cache-Control: no-cache` for its page and scripts, said in its own headers and not left to the host, and every console has the control that offers the latest to a page left open. Never reload under someone in a room, in a headset or writing; never clear a visitor's storage, sign-in or settings to make an update take; and never lengthen what a browser may keep without asking, except for a file named by its hash.
- **The crossing's room in an engine (sdk `engine-crossing.ts`, `engines/godot/addons/nswp/web/shell.html`; asked for by the owner on 2026-11-20: "a user to move from a 3JS world into a gdot world with as little friction or disturbance as possible"):** a world made in another engine shows the crossing's room from its page's first moment and never the engine's own loading screen, and brings the room up again before leaving. It is the page's picture of the room, with nothing in it to press, saying "verified" about nothing. Never put an engine's name or logo back in front of a visitor between worlds, and never let the room ask for or show anything the Airlock is there to check.
- **Tabs kept on the bar (sdk `ui/model.ts` `setPinned`, core `settings.ts` `pins`; asked for by the owner on 2026-11-19, who approved it travelling):** a visitor pins a tab of the menu to their bar from the menu, four at most, and it goes with them as a setting. Only they pin: never pin for a visitor from a world's code, never put more on the bar by default to make up for it, and never let `pins` carry anything but short plain names (`PIN_NAME`). A tab a world doesn't have is kept and passed on, never dropped.
- **"Signed in as" (sdk `ui/hud.ts`, `sayWho`; owner's instruction of 2026-11-19):** the badge at the top centre is said once when who the visitor is here changes, from the card at the head of You and from nothing else. It is never shown for someone who isn't signed in here, never made pressable, and never left up: it comes and goes where it stands.
- **A backup for relays (core `relay-discovery.ts`, sdk `relay-book.ts`; NIP-66; the owner's ask of 2026-11-20: "add relay nip-66 as an option across the board. So there is always a backup for relays"):** when everything a person chose and the usual relays have failed, a few relays that monitors found working are tried. Keep it a last resort: never ask monitors while anything chosen is answering, never put a found relay before a person's own, and never write a visitor's relay list from it. `pickDiscovered` takes a relay only where two monitors agree and none says it asks for proof, payment or work: don't lower `DISCOVERY.agree` to one, and don't pin trust to a single monitor's key. Never send a wrapped message (1059) to a backup. Nothing may depend on it: with no monitor answering, every call behaves as before. It never stands in for relays that were never named (an empty list sends nowhere), and it is off outside a browser unless the caller injects the query or publish: on 2026-11-20 a BitPortal unit test that sent to an empty list published a made-up rating to two real relays through it. Don't relax `mayBackUp`.
- **Which relays (core `relay.ts`: `pickRelays`; sdk `relay-book.ts`; the owner's ask of 2026-11-20: "always pushes notes to the best one"):** a person's own write relays are always used, however they have been answering: it is where others look for them. What the kit learns only orders them and decides whether the usual relays are added. Don't drop an own relay for a low score, don't write to a visitor's relay list (10002 stays "careful"), and keep what is remembered about relays only: never which person or world was asked about.
- **People and messages (NSWP-14; core `people.ts`, `message.ts`; sdk `people.ts`; asked for by the owner on 2026-11-20: "following people, unfollow people and DMs"):** these are Nostr's own (NIP-02, NIP-17). `changeFollows` starts from the newest list found and keeps every tag and the content it doesn't understand; it refuses with no list unless told the person follows nobody (`fromNothing`), because "none found" may be a relay that was down. Don't make that the default: the kit writes a first list only when relays answered with none and the visitor pressed twice. `readMessage` drops a message whose writer isn't the key that signed its seal: that check is what stops one person writing as another. A seal's time is now, not moved back: BitLogin replaces a time more than ten minutes off, and BitPortal's `checkedSigner` would then refuse what came back.
- **Scores and achievements on a card (sdk `person-lines.ts`; the owner's ask of 2026-11-20: "achievements and leaderboard info on profiles"):** a world fills a person's card only from its own boards and achievements, which it asked relays for by world. Don't have a world ask a relay about the person on a card: that rule of NSWP-10 stands. A visitor's own client may ask about a person, when its visitor opens their card, and shows only what is marked with the address of a world whose announcement it has. Every line is a claim: word it as what they say or show, never as fact.
- **Achievements (NSWP-16; core `achievement.ts`; sdk `achievements.ts`, `achieving.ts`; asked for by the owner on 2026-11-20):** built as scores are, and for the same reasons. Optional: a world gets them only by naming some. What a visitor earned is kept on the device and shown as theirs only from their own press. An achievement shown is a claim: never word one as verified or granted; a granted one (a NIP-58 badge from a service a reader trusts) is a later layer. It counts only with the signer's mark for the world's own address (`achievementsIn` needs `worldUrl`). A hidden one is never named to someone who hasn't earned it. `sign` refuses kind 37307: the signer writes the note itself (`achieve`, `achievementForWorld`).
- **Scores (NSWP-15; core `score.ts`; sdk `scores.ts`, `scoring.ts`; the owner's asks of 2026-11-20: scores and leaderboards without a validator first, and "all this should be optional parts of our kit that worlds can interop with if they want to"):** optional throughout: a world gets scores only by naming boards, and nothing about them appears otherwise. Don't add a Scores tab, a board or a request to relays to a world that named none. A score is a claim: never word one as verified, a record or a win; a checked score is a later layer (BitUnlock) over this one. A score counts only if the signer's mark is the world's own address (`rankScores` needs `worldUrl`): don't make that optional. A visitor's best is kept on the device and posted only from their own press, because a posted score says publicly where they were. Boards are asked of relays by world (`#a`), never by person. `sign` refuses kind 37306: the signer writes a score itself (`score`, `scoreForWorld`).
- **Messages (sdk `messages.ts`, `messaging.ts`, `ui/letters.ts`; core `message.ts`; the owner's word of 2026-11-20: "more like a nostr client"):** a world its visitor has let (`messages`) sends and reads their private messages through the signer: `seal`, `unwrap`, `reach`. Don't give a world raw encrypting or decrypting, don't let `sign` take 13, 14 or 1059 (`NEVER_KINDS` stands), and don't add a way for a world to get at a seal's contents. The conversation is its own side of the chat panel, named for who it is with, and says who can read it where the words are typed: never merge it with the room's chat or the public feed, where a word typed in the wrong one is public. Nothing read is kept on the device, only how far each conversation was read. A message from someone the visitor doesn't follow and hasn't written to is a request: listed apart, never counted, never announced. Nobody is sent a message who has no kind 10050, and the kit never falls back to somewhere else.
- **Following, in the menu (sdk `following.ts`, `profiles.ts`; the owner's word of 2026-11-20: "more like a nostr client"):** the tab is the kit's, added by `addRoomControls`, so it is the same in every world and at a visitor's home. Don't build a second one in a client. It shows nothing and asks no relay anything until the visitor has let that place see to who they follow. Names are asked for a page at a time, for the people on show: don't fetch a whole follow list's profiles at once. A name proves nothing; the card shows the key.
- **Arrow keys (owner's ask of 2026-11-20):** on a flat screen, the casual way, left and right turn and up and down walk; A and D step to the side. `KEY_TURN` is the rate. Game controls (`fps.ts`) are separate and were not changed.
- **A world may ask for more (NSWP-10, "Asking for more"; core `SIGNER_MORE`, `followForWorld`; the owner's decision of 2026-11-20: "We need to start thinking of worlds no differently than a Nostr client. Some trust is required."):** a world its visitor has let can follow and unfollow for them, from a person's card. Keep the shape of it: nothing more by default; asked for by name from a closed list (`follows` today), in the signer's own window, one world at a time; asked when the visitor presses what needs it, never on arrival. A world never builds the list: it hands over the visitor's own signed list and one change (`follow`), and the kit checks what comes back is exactly that (`isFollowAsked`). Don't widen `ORDINARY_KINDS`, don't let `sign` take kind 3, and don't add a name that means "sign whatever the world hands over". The kit asks no relay who a visitor follows until they have let that world. `NEVER_KINDS` stands; messages from a world would be another name on the list, with the signer sealing and opening each one, and are not built.
- **`/xr-test/` on every world is a test page, not a feature** (`sites/shared/xr-test/`; the owner, 2026-11-21: "I want VR mode to be absolutely seamless... let's at least get Quest 3s working well"). It finds out what a headset's browser really does at a door: whether it carries VR to the next site (`sessiongranted`), with the page staying in VR or leaving it first, by a middle page, and inside BitPortal's frame. It uses no kit code, stores nothing and sends nothing; its log rides in the address after the `#`. What it found decides three things that are NOT yet done: `portal.go` still ends the headset session before a door, the Airlock has no headset view to take a session carried to it, and nobody knows yet whether a framed world is carried at all. Don't change any of those on a guess: ask what the owner's Quest showed. Take the page out once those are settled.
- **A filled screen that stays filled is a trial (kit 0.61.0; `protocol/02-doors.md`, "A client that keeps the screen"; BitPortal `/shell/`; the owner's "Yes, prototype it" of 2026-11-20, for our own worlds only):** a reference world lets the client it sends visitors through frame it (`frame-ancestors 'self' <client>` in `build-sites.mjs`, `build-godot-example.mjs` and the template's build), and nobody else. Never widen that to `*` or `https:`: a page that frames a world can lay something over it. The locked box is served with `itemBoxCsp(<that client>)` in those worlds, because every page above a frame has to be named by it and the box is then under the client too: it adds that one origin to `frame-ancestors` and nothing to what the box may reach, and refuses anything that isn't one plain https origin. Don't add to the box's rules any other way. Don't make any world depend on being framed, don't hand a framed world the screen (`fullscreen` is not delegated, so the kit's own fullscreen code is inert there and can't collapse the client's), and don't treat any of this as protocol for other people's worlds until the owner says the trial is kept. Since 0.62.0 a framed world asks the page above it for the screen (`client-frame.ts`: `here`, `screen`), and says nothing else to it: keep those words free of anything about the visitor, take words only from `window.parent`, and never let a framed world fill the screen itself. Since 0.63.0 it asks the same page for the sign-in address's panel in place of the window (`sign-in`; the owner, 2026-11-21: "Keep all of it in app and not in a separate window"): `signer.unlock()` is the one place that chooses, so don't open the sign-in window from anywhere else. A world never frames that panel itself and never widens what it frames for it: the client draws it, above the world. Use the panel only when the client's `signIn` is this world's own signer's real address, and keep the window as the fallback (no frame, no panel, another signer, a panel that failed to come up). Since 0.64.0 a world can put a careful kind (0, 5, 10002: `PROPOSE_KINDS`, a closed list; never 3, never a never-kind) or a wallet to the visitor themselves (`signer.propose`, `signer.wallet`; NSWP-10, "Asking the person"): the signer answers `asking`, the sign-in address's own page is shown, and nothing is signed or handed over but at the visitor's press there. Never make `sign` take those kinds, never add a kind to the list without the owner, always check what comes back as `propose` does, and treat a wallet connection as spending power: no reference world asks for one yet, and one that does must ask the owner first how it is kept. Since 0.65.0 a world can ask to be paid instead (`signer.pay(invoice)`): one invoice, one press, and the world learns only that it was paid. Prefer that to a wallet for anything that is a payment. Never treat `paid` without `proven` as proof for handing over anything of value, never read more from an invoice than `invoice.ts` does to decide anything, and add no world feature that takes money without the owner. The first use of `propose` is deleting your own post (0.66.0, `removeNote` in `room-controls.ts`; the owner, 2026-11-21, on which feature first: "Whatever order it wants"). - **The crossing's room (`packages/sdk/src/chamber.ts`):** it is drawn last, on a cleared depth (`CHAMBER_ORDER`), so that nothing a world or another visitor has standing there shows inside it. Don't give anything else a draw order at or above it but the visitor's own things (`keep`), and don't draw other people while it stands.
- **Moving on a flat screen (`packages/sdk/src/stroll.ts`):** a tap or click is a walk at the keys' pace to a spot `TAP_REACH` metres off at most. Don't turn it back into a jump or lengthen it without the owner's decision. In a headset a step stays a jump: a slide there makes people ill. A press while walking adds to the path from where it ended, and two presses close together are a run straight there with the rest forgotten (the owner's instruction of 2026-11-19: "click out a path as we are walking"): don't make a single press start again from the visitor's feet. The view faces the way they walk (`stroll.face`), eased and never faster than `FACE_RATE`: never turn anyone's view in a headset, never while they are dragging it themselves, and never snap it.
- **Who a world is for (NSWP-01, NSWP-08, spec §15):** `audience` is `kids`, `general` or `adult`, and `audienceOf` reads a warning with no audience as adult. Never make that reading more trusting (a warned, unrated world taken as fine for everyone), never let `adult` be announced without `content-warning`, and never describe a rating as a safeguard on its own: it is the maker's word, and the safeguard is a host's list. Change the tag and its document, vectors and `@nswp/core` change together.
- **A home someone starts in (NSWP-07, spec §14.1):** `home` in a manifest's capabilities, `doors=1` on an Airlock with no destination, and `home` in the arrival context belong together: change one and the other two, their documents, the arrival schema and its vectors change with it. A world that says it can be a home must keep the way to the visitor's doors wherever it offers the way home.
- `browser/spec.md` is a plan for a browser, not part of the protocol. A world must never need that browser: nothing in `protocol/` may depend on it, and no announcement field is added for it until one exists to read it.
